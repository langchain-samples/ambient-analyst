# Oil market note (verified 2026-07-20)

## Current official signals
- EIA July 2026 STEO / press release (2026-07-07): Brent averages $82/b in 2026 and $65/b in 2027; U.S. crude production 13.8 mb/d in 2026 and 14.0 mb/d in 2027; Brent averaged $85/b in June and EIA expects continued inventory builds to push prices lower over the next year. Source: https://www.eia.gov/pressroom/releases/press590.php
- IEA Oil Market Report (2026-07): world demand recovery is underway, but 2026 demand is still projected to decline by about 1 mb/d overall; global supply rebounded 4.1 mb/d to 98.8 mb/d in June after Strait of Hormuz flows resumed; supply is still about 9.4 mb/d below pre-war levels; if transit volumes normalize, supply could expand by 7.5 mb/d next year. Source: https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-july-2026
- OPEC site (2026-07): OPEC announced a 5 July 2026 production adjustment by Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman to support market stability; OPEC also launched World Oil Outlook 2026 and continues to frame demand as rising toward 124 mb/d by 2050. Source: https://www.opec.org

## Durable takeaway
- Over the next 12 months, oil pricing is likely to be driven more by geopolitics, refinery disruption, and spare-capacity normalization than by structural demand growth.
- Near-term downside comes from higher global supply, inventory builds, and softer product cracks once summer demand fades.
- Main upside risks are renewed Strait of Hormuz disruption, faster OPEC+ discipline, or a sharper-than-expected demand rebound.
